Army, Air or Sea Cadets: Which to Join?

Four organisations, a lot of overlap, and one genuinely important deciding factor that most people overlook.

The four you will come across

  • Army Cadet Force (ACF) — community-based detachments meeting locally in the evenings. Fieldcraft, navigation, drill, first aid, adventurous training, shooting.
  • RAF Air Cadets (including the Air Training Corps) — aviation-focused: flying and gliding opportunities, principles of flight, radio and navigation, alongside general cadet training.
  • Sea Cadets — maritime-focused: boating, sailing, kayaking, seamanship and navigation, with a nautical structure and traditions.
  • Combined Cadet Force (CCF) — school-based, and different in kind: it runs inside a school or college and may have Army, RAF and Royal Navy sections under one contingent.

What they have in common

Far more than the branding suggests. All four are youth organisations built around progressive training, teamwork, leadership and adventure. All offer the Duke of Edinburgh’s Award and recognised qualifications. None commits a young person to joining the armed forces. The core experience — a weekly evening, weekends away, an annual camp, gradually taking on responsibility — is broadly similar.

The factor most people overlook

Which unit is actually near you, and whether it has space. In practice the choice is often made for you by geography: a brilliant Sea Cadet unit forty minutes away with a waiting list is a worse option than a good ACF detachment ten minutes down the road that can take your child next week. Travel every week, in the dark, in February, is the thing that quietly ends cadet careers.

If your child is at a school with a CCF, that is usually the path of least resistance, because it happens where they already are.

How to choose

  • Start with what is genuinely reachable on a weekly basis, all year round.
  • Then consider the interest. A child fixated on aircraft will get more from Air Cadets; one who loves water will get more from Sea Cadets.
  • Then go and look. The people running the unit matter more than the badge on the door. Almost every unit will let you visit before committing.

Joining ages vary between the organisations and by area, so confirm locally rather than relying on a general figure. Our joining ages guide covers the ACF and CCF specifically.

Quick answers

Choosing between cadet forces: FAQs

What is the difference between the ACF and the CCF?
The Army Cadet Force is community-based: you join a local detachment that meets in the evenings. The Combined Cadet Force runs inside a school or college and may have Army, RAF and Royal Navy sections in one contingent.
Which cadet organisation is best?
None is objectively best. The most important practical factor is which unit is genuinely reachable every week all year round, and whether it has space, because weekly travel is what quietly ends cadet careers. After that, follow the young person's interest, then go and visit.
Do you have to join the armed forces afterwards?
No. None of the cadet organisations commits a young person to joining the armed forces, and most cadets do not. They are youth organisations focused on personal development, teamwork and adventure.
Does Cadet AI work for Air Cadets or Sea Cadets?
Not really. Cadet AI is built around the Army Cadet Force and CCF syllabus. Air Cadets and Sea Cadets follow different training syllabuses, so the app would be much less useful to them.
