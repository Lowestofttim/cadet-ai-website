The Duke of Edinburgh’s Award and Cadets

One of the most useful things about cadets is that a lot of what cadets already do can count towards DofE. Here’s how the two fit together.

Why the two fit together so well

The Duke of Edinburgh’s Award asks young people to commit to activities across four sections over a period of months. Cadet units are often licensed DofE centres, and much of what a cadet does anyway — learning skills, staying active, helping out, going on expeditions — maps directly onto those sections. In practice that means a cadet is frequently part-way through DofE without having set out to be.

The four sections

  • Volunteering — giving time to help others. Community engagement activities through the unit often count.
  • Physical — sport or any activity that improves fitness. Cadet sport and physical training can count.
  • Skills — developing a practical or personal talent. Many cadet syllabus subjects fit here.
  • Expedition — planning, training for and completing a journey. This is where cadet navigation and fieldcraft training pays off directly.

At Gold there is a fifth requirement, a residential: a shared activity away from home with people you do not know.

Bronze, Silver and Gold

The three levels differ in how long you must sustain each section and in the demands of the expedition, with Gold being the longest commitment and the most demanding journey. Age guidelines apply to each level, and your unit or DofE leader will confirm what applies to you.

Cadets who start at Bronze and keep going often reach Gold before they leave, which is a genuinely substantial thing to have done by 18.

Why it is worth doing

DofE is widely recognised by employers and universities, and unlike many things on a personal statement it is externally verified. More practically, the expedition section teaches planning, navigation and dealing with things going wrong in a way that classroom learning does not.

How to start

Ask at your detachment or contingent. Availability, timing and which levels are run vary between units, so the answer is local. If your unit does not currently run it, schools and other local DofE centres do.

Quick answers

DofE and cadets: FAQs

Do all cadet units offer the Duke of Edinburgh's Award?
Many do, as cadet units are often licensed DofE centres, but availability and which levels are run vary by unit. Ask your detachment or school contingent, and if they do not run it, schools and other local DofE centres do.
Can cadet activities count towards DofE sections?
Yes, frequently. Volunteering, physical activity, skills and expedition all have natural equivalents in cadet training, which is why cadets often find they are already part-way through. Your DofE leader confirms what counts.
What are the four DofE sections?
Volunteering, Physical, Skills and Expedition. At Gold level there is an additional residential requirement: a shared activity away from home with people you do not already know.
Is DofE worth doing?
It is widely recognised by employers and universities, and unlike much of what goes on a personal statement it is externally verified. The expedition section in particular teaches planning and navigation in a way classroom learning does not.
