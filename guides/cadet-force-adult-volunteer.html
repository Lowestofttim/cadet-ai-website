Becoming a Cadet Force Adult Volunteer

Cadet units run on adult volunteers, and most had no military background before they started. Here’s what the role actually involves and how you join.

You do not need a military background

This is the single most common misconception. Most Cadet Force Adult Volunteers (CFAVs) come in with no service experience at all. What units actually need is reliable adults who are good with young people and willing to be trained — the cadet-specific knowledge is taught to you.

What the role involves

Volunteers deliver and supervise training, run parade nights, take cadets on weekends and camps, and provide the consistent adult presence the whole thing depends on. Some volunteers specialise — adventurous training, shooting, first aid, administration — and some are generalists. There is more variety in it than people expect.

Requirements

  • Age: adults 18 and over. Exact minimums for particular roles are set by the organisation.
  • Vetting: you will go through safeguarding checks, including a criminal records check. This is non-negotiable and rightly so.
  • References and an interview as part of joining.
  • Training: an induction plus ongoing courses. Much of it is genuinely transferable — first aid, leadership, instructional technique.

Specifics vary between the ACF and a school-based CCF, and between counties. The unit or county headquarters will tell you exactly what applies.

Time commitment

Typically one parade night a week during term, plus some weekends and, if you can, annual camp. Units understand that volunteers have jobs and families, and most would far rather have someone reliable one night a week than someone sporadic. Being honest about your availability up front is better than over-promising.

What you get out of it

Recognised qualifications, genuinely useful leadership and instructional experience, and the specific satisfaction of watching a quiet fourteen-year-old turn into someone who can lead a section. Many volunteers say it is the most rewarding thing they do.

How to apply

  • ACF: contact your county headquarters or a local detachment through the official Army Cadets website.
  • CCF: contact the school or college that runs the contingent. Some CCF roles are open to non-teaching staff and parents.

Quick answers

Volunteering: FAQs

Do you need to have been in the military to volunteer with cadets?
No. Most Cadet Force Adult Volunteers have no service background. Units need reliable adults who are good with young people and willing to be trained; the cadet-specific knowledge is taught to you.
What checks do adult volunteers go through?
Safeguarding vetting including a criminal records check, plus references and an interview as part of joining. Exact processes are set by the organisation and county.
How much time does volunteering take?
Typically one parade night a week in term time, plus some weekends and annual camp if you can manage it. Units would rather have someone reliable one night a week than someone sporadic, so be honest about availability.
How do you apply to become a CFAV?
For the ACF, contact your county headquarters or a local detachment via the official Army Cadets website. For a school-based CCF, contact the school or college running the contingent.
