Cadet Uniform & Inspection

Turnout is the first thing anyone judges and the easiest thing to fix. Here’s what inspectors actually look for, and where your effort pays off most.

What an inspection is actually for

Inspection is not about perfection for its own sake. It checks that you can prepare properly, pay attention to detail and turn up ready — the same habits the rest of the training relies on. Which is why consistent, tidy effort scores better than one heroic session the night before.

The quick wins, in order of payoff

  • Be clean and tidy in yourself. Hair to your unit’s standard, clean hands and nails. This costs nothing and is noticed immediately.
  • Iron the obvious surfaces. Sharp creases where they belong, no crumples on the front. You do not need to iron every square inch to look sharp.
  • Boots. Clean and polished beats scuffed every time, and a decent shine on the toecap does most of the visual work.
  • Badges and insignia straight and in the right place. A crooked badge undoes a lot of ironing.
  • Loose threads. Take thirty seconds with scissors. Inspectors always spot them.

Boots

Getting a shine takes layers and patience rather than force: thin coats of polish, a little water, and a soft cloth, built up over several sessions. Trying to do it all at once produces a dull, streaky mess. Do a little each week and boots stay easy to bring back up.

Keep the welts and edges clean too — a gleaming toecap above a muddy sole rim still reads as scruffy.

Looking after it between parade nights

  • Hang uniform up rather than leaving it in a bag. Most creases are avoidable.
  • Deal with mud once it is dry, not while it is wet.
  • Follow the care instructions on the garment — some items should not be washed or ironed like ordinary clothes.
  • Report damage or a poor fit to your unit. Uniform is issued and exchanged; you are not expected to soldier on in boots that do not fit.

One important caveat

Exact standards — which items are worn, where badges sit, what is expected for a particular parade — vary between counties, contingents and occasions, and your instructors set them. This guide covers the general principles; where your unit differs, your unit is right.

Quick answers

Uniform and turnout: FAQs

What do inspectors look for in a cadet inspection?
Personal turnout, uniform that is clean and pressed, boots that are clean and polished, badges straight and correctly placed, and no loose threads. Consistent tidy effort scores better than one heroic session the night before.
How do you shine cadet boots?
Thin layers of polish built up over several sessions with a little water and a soft cloth, rather than trying to do it all at once, which produces a dull streaky finish. Keep the welts and sole edges clean as well as the toecap.
Do cadets have to buy their own uniform?
No. Uniform is issued by the unit and exchanged as cadets grow or if it is damaged. Report a poor fit rather than putting up with it.
Are uniform standards the same everywhere?
No. Which items are worn, where badges sit and what is expected for a given parade vary between counties, contingents and occasions. Your instructors set the standard for your unit.
